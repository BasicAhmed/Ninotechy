How Much Does a Website Cost in Qatar, UAE, Saudi Arabia & Malaysia?

Website pricing across the Gulf and Southeast Asia varies more by scope than by country — here's what actually moves the number.

"How much does a website cost?" is close to impossible to answer without knowing what the website has to do. A five-page brochure site and a booking platform with a backend admin panel are not the same product, even if they're both called "a website." That said, regional pricing patterns are real, and it helps to know roughly where the market sits.

What actually drives the price

  • Number of unique page templates — not page count, but how many different layouts need to be designed
  • Custom functionality — bookings, quote systems, multi-language support, payment integration
  • Content readiness — writing and sourcing content from scratch takes real time
  • Design from scratch vs. template customization
  • Ongoing needs — hosting, maintenance, and future updates

Typical ranges by project type

These are general market ranges seen across Qatar, the UAE, Saudi Arabia and Malaysia — actual quotes depend heavily on scope:

  • Simple brochure website (5–8 pages): roughly $800–$2,500
  • Business website with custom design and light functionality: roughly $2,500–$6,000
  • Booking, quotation, or e-commerce platform: roughly $5,000–$15,000+
  • Custom web application with an admin dashboard: $10,000 and up, scaling with complexity

Regional notes

Qatar and the UAE tend to sit at the higher end of these ranges, partly due to demand for bilingual (Arabic/English) builds and higher expectations around visual polish for hospitality, real estate, and luxury sectors.

Saudi Arabia has seen fast-growing demand tied to Vision 2030 initiatives, with many businesses investing earlier in stronger digital presence, which pushes budgets up for e-commerce and service platforms specifically.

Malaysia generally offers a wider spread of pricing, with strong competition among local agencies keeping entry-level pricing more accessible, while custom platforms for fintech, education, and logistics still command premium pricing similar to the Gulf.

Where businesses overspend — and underspend

Overspending usually happens when a simple brochure site is built with unnecessary custom backend logic "for future flexibility" that never gets used. Underspending usually shows up later as a site that can't be updated without a developer, or one that ranks poorly because SEO fundamentals were skipped to hit a lower price.

A better question than "how much"

The more useful question is: what does this website need to do in the next 12 months? Pricing a website against a real scope — not a vague idea — is what keeps a quote accurate and a project on budget.
